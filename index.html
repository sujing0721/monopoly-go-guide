Getting Started · Board Stages

Getting Started

A lot of new players use the same playbook from board 1 to board 20. In reality, priorities shift as new mechanics unlock. Knowing which stage you're in matters more than mastering any single trick.

Boards 1–4

Learn the rhythm

Complete property sets to build a baseline income, get comfortable with Shutdowns and Bank Heists, and start checking the event calendar daily until it's a habit.

Mid boards

Start being deliberate

Stop spending dice on autopilot — save them for high-multiplier events. Start following sticker trading groups; trading beats grinding for rare stickers most of the time.

Late game / Landmarks

Concentrate resources

Once Landmarks, Peg-E, and Partner Events unlock, the strategy shifts from "steady growth" to "stack resources for key events." Shields and defense matter a lot more here — see the attack & defense guide.

Building & Property Strategy

Build Order

Upgrade order directly affects your cash flow and how much you lose to a Shutdown — don't max out the first building you see.

Common Mistakes — and What to Do Instead

Mistakes

Most players plateau because of a handful of repeated mistakes, not because they're unlucky. Here are the ones we see most often, and the specific fix for each.

Mistake 1

Rolling dice the moment you get them

It feels productive to spend dice right away, but it's almost always a net loss over time. Dice have no value when spent outside a boosted window — a single 2x multiplier event can double your return on every roll. Get into the habit of letting your dice bank build up, then only tap it when you know there's an active multiplier or a high-value event running. Even waiting just 12–24 hours makes a meaningful difference. The full routine is in our dice strategy guide.

Mistake 2

Upgrading the wrong building first

New players tend to upgrade whichever property they land on most, which is often a high-cost building that won't pay for itself for many boards. The better rule: always finish a full property set before pushing any single building to high tiers. Set completion bonuses are significant and unlock immediately, whereas individual building tiers pay out slowly. If you find yourself with 3 out of 4 properties on a set, get that last one — don't keep upgrading a random standalone.

Mistake 3

Ignoring the friends list

The single biggest free source of dice in the game is friend gifts, and most players have far fewer friends than they should. A full friends list (200 slots) generates dice daily just from the gift mechanic alone — no effort required beyond accepting them. Join at least one active trading community, swap friend codes regularly, and prune inactive accounts so you're not wasting slots. This alone can add several hundred dice per week for completely free — more sources on our free dice page.

Mistake 4

Leaving cash exposed before logging off

Shutdowns and Bank Heists target your cash and buildings while you're away. There's no reason to log off with a large, unspent cash pile — spend it on upgrades before you go, even if it means upgrading something you didn't have planned. You'll lose less value from a slightly early upgrade than from a successful Shutdown against a big target. If you can't spend it, use it on Peg-E tokens or quick event entries. The full playbook is in the attack & defense guide.

Mistake 5

Grinding for stickers instead of trading

Players who pull packs hoping to find rare stickers are running the slowest possible strategy. The rarest stickers have very low pack odds, so the expected number of packs you'd need is enormous. A single active trading group with 50–100 members will have someone who has what you need and wants what you have. Trading takes seconds; grinding takes weeks. Even if you only have commons to offer, they're valuable to the specific people who need them for a set — the sticker trading guide covers where and how to trade safely.

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ
Q · A

Is it better to save dice or spend them steadily?

Save them for multipliers. A steady trickle of 100 dice per day spent on normal rolls gives you a baseline return, but the same 100 dice spent during a 2x or 3x event can yield double or triple the rewards. The one exception is if you're still on early boards where every roll advances your progression meaningfully regardless of multipliers — there, a balanced approach works fine.

Q · A

Are free dice links legitimate?

Some are. Scopely and community content creators occasionally share legitimate dice codes through official social channels. However, most websites claiming to offer "free dice generators" are clickbait or data-harvesting schemes. A legitimate dice link gives you a code that the game validates; it doesn't ask you to download anything or enter your login credentials. If a free-dice site asks for your account info, it's a scam. We keep a full rundown of trustworthy sources on the free dice page.

Q · A

How often does the event calendar reset?

The event rotation is on an ongoing weekly cycle — there isn't a fixed monthly reset. Different event types recur on roughly a 5–7 day cycle, but they overlap and stagger, so there's almost always something active. The most important thing isn't memorising exact dates (those change) but learning to recognise the rhythm — our events guide breaks down each recurring type and how to prepare.

Q · A

What's the best way to find sticker trading partners?

The fastest routes are dedicated Discord servers for MONOPOLY GO! and Facebook groups specifically for the game. Reddit's r/MonopolyGO also has a trading thread. When you join, post your code once and keep it updated — the most active players will reach out. Having a short list of "I need" and "I have" makes it much easier for others to match with you quickly, rather than just saying "anyone want to trade." The sticker guide has the full trading checklist.

Q · A

Do shields actually protect my cash?

Yes, fully. While a shield is active, Bank Heist attempts against your account will fail automatically. The limitation is that shields have a timer — typically lasting a few hours — so you can't just activate one reactively when you see an attack coming. The strategic use is to activate one when you're sitting on cash you haven't had time to spend, such as before logging off at night or during a busy workday when you can't monitor your account.
